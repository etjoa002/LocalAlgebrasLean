\section*{Nets}
Let $R$ be a preordered set of regions and $A$ a C$^*$-algebra. A star subalgebra is a unital complex star subalgebra, and a star isomorphism is a unital complex $*$-isomorphism. A region index on $R$ consists of an orthogonality relation $O_1 \perp O_2$ on the regions.

\paragraph{1.} Region indices and their isomorphisms. A relation $r$ on $R$ is the orthogonality relation of a region index exactly when it is symmetric and antitone in its first argument ($O_0 \le O_1$ and $r(O_1, O_2)$ give $r(O_0, O_2)$), and a region index is determined by its relation. For region indices $I$ on $R$ and $I'$ on a preordered set $R'$, with orthogonality relations $\perp$ and $\perp'$, an isomorphism from $I$ to $I'$ has a map $R \to R'$; a map $f$ is the map of an isomorphism exactly when it is the map of an order isomorphism and $f(O_1) \perp' f(O_2) \iff O_1 \perp O_2$ for all regions $O_1, O_2$, and an isomorphism is determined by its map. For a set $V$ of sites, the region index of the subsets of $V$, ordered by inclusion, has two subsets orthogonal exactly when they are disjoint.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.region\_index\_iff}}

\paragraph{2.} Nets and generated nets. A net over $R$ in $A$ assigns to each region $O$ a star subalgebra $\mathcal A(O)$ of $A$; an assignment $O \mapsto F(O)$ of star subalgebras is a net exactly when it is monotone ($O \le O'$ implies $F(O) \subseteq F(O')$, isotony) and every $F(O)$ is norm-closed, and a net is determined by its algebras. For a monotone family $O \mapsto G(O)$ of subsets of $A$, the generated net assigns to each region $O$ the norm closure of the star subalgebra of $A$ generated by $G(O)$ (a unital one, so that it contains the unit, and is $\mathbb C 1$ when $G(O)$ is empty).\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.net\_iff}}

\paragraph{3.} Properties of nets. Let $\mathcal A$ be a net over $R$ in $A$ and $I$ a region index on $R$ with orthogonality $\perp$. Generation: $\mathcal A$ is generating exactly when the norm closure of the star subalgebra generated by all its algebras is $A$. Local commutativity: $\mathcal A$ is locally commutative for $I$ exactly when, whenever $O_1 \perp O_2$, every element of $\mathcal A(O_1)$ commutes with every element of $\mathcal A(O_2)$. Covariance: let $\mathcal A'$ be a net over a preordered set $R'$ in a C$^*$-algebra $A'$, $I'$ a region index on $R'$, $\kappa$ an isomorphism from $I$ to $I'$ and $\alpha : A \to A'$ a star isomorphism; then $(\kappa, \alpha)$ is a covariance pair of $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal A'$ exactly when $\alpha(\mathcal A(O)) = \mathcal A'(\kappa(O))$ for every region $O$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.net\_properties\_iff}}

\paragraph{4.} Nets generated by sets. Let $O \mapsto G(O)$ be a monotone family of subsets of $A$ over $R$, with generated net $\mathcal A$. If the norm closure of the star subalgebra generated by the union of all $G(O)$ is $A$, then $\mathcal A$ is generating. Let $I$ be a region index on $R$ with orthogonality $\perp$: if every $G(O)$ is closed under the adjoint and every element of $G(O_1)$ commutes with every element of $G(O_2)$ whenever $O_1 \perp O_2$, then $\mathcal A$ is locally commutative for $I$. Let $I'$ be a region index on a preordered set $R'$, $A'$ a C$^*$-algebra, $O \mapsto G'(O)$ a monotone family of subsets of $A'$ over $R'$ with generated net $\mathcal A'$, $\kappa$ an isomorphism from $I$ to $I'$ and $\alpha : A \to A'$ a star isomorphism with $\alpha(G(O)) = G'(\kappa(O))$ for every region $O$: then $(\kappa, \alpha)$ is a covariance pair of $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal A'$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.generated\_net\_properties}}

\section*{The qudit net}
Let $\Lambda$ be a finite set of sites, each site $u$ carrying a finite alphabet $\sigma_u$; the alphabets may differ from site to site. A configuration $s$ chooses a letter $s(u) \in \sigma_u$ at every site. The qudit algebra is the algebra of complex matrices indexed by the configurations, with the matrix product and the conjugate transpose, a C$^*$-algebra with the operator norm of the matrix acting on the Euclidean space of complex functions on the configurations. For a set of sites $X$, a matrix on the letters of $X$ is a complex matrix indexed by the assignments of a letter in $\sigma_x$ to each $x \in X$, and $s|_X$ is the restriction of a configuration $s$ to $X$.

\paragraph{5.} The qudit algebra, the embedding and the qudit net. A configuration is a function assigning to each site $u$ a letter in $\sigma_u$, and the qudit algebra consists of the complex matrices indexed by the configurations. For a set of sites $X$ and a matrix $m$ on the letters of $X$, the embedding $j_X(m)$ is the matrix with \[j_X(m)_{s s'} = m_{s|_X, s'|_X}\] if the configurations $s$ and $s'$ agree at every site outside $X$, and $0$ otherwise. The qudit net is a net over the sets of sites in the qudit algebra; a matrix $M$ lies in its algebra $\mathcal A(X)$ of a set of sites $X$ exactly when (1) $M_{s s'} = 0$ whenever $s$ and $s'$ differ at some site outside $X$, and (2) $M_{s s'} = M_{r r'}$ whenever $s$, $s'$ agree outside $X$, $r$, $r'$ agree outside $X$, $s = r$ on $X$ and $s' = r'$ on $X$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.qudit\_net}}

\paragraph{6.} If every alphabet is nonempty, then for every set of sites $X$ the algebra $\mathcal A(X)$ of the qudit net consists exactly of the matrices $j_X(m)$ with $m$ a matrix on the letters of $X$. The algebra $\mathcal A(\Lambda)$ of the set of all sites is the whole qudit algebra. Local commutativity: the qudit net is locally commutative for the region index of the subsets of $\Lambda$, in which two sets of sites are orthogonal exactly when they are disjoint.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.qudit\_net\_range\_and\_locality}}

\paragraph{7.} Relabelling covariance. Let $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda'$ be finite sets of sites with one finite alphabet $Q$ at every site, and $e : \Lambda \to \Lambda'$ a bijection. The relabelling by $e$ is a star isomorphism $\rho_e$ from the qudit algebra of $\Lambda$ onto the qudit algebra of $\Lambda'$ with \[(\rho_e M)_{t t'} = M_{t \circ e, t' \circ e}\] for every matrix $M$ in the qudit algebra of $\Lambda$ and all configurations $t, t'$ of $\Lambda'$; and $\rho_e(\mathcal A(X)) = \mathcal A'(e(X))$ for every set of sites $X \subseteq \Lambda$, where $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal A'$ are the qudit nets of $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda'$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.relabel\_covariance}}

\section*{Setting}
There are sites $\Lambda$ (any set, possibly infinite), a finite alphabet $\sigma_u$ at every site $u$, and a reference configuration $s_0$ choosing a letter at every site (so every alphabet is nonempty). For a finite set of sites $X$, a matrix on the letters of $X$ is a complex matrix indexed by the assignments $\xi$ of a letter $\xi_x \in \sigma_x$ to each $x \in X$, that is, an operator on $\bigotimes_{x \in X} \mathbb C^{\sigma_x}$. Bounded operators carry the operator norm, and C$^*$-algebras are complex.

\paragraph{8.} The finitely supported configurations are the configurations $s$ that differ from the reference configuration $s_0$ at finitely many sites.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.finConfig\_def}}

\paragraph{9.} The Hilbert space $H$ is $\ell^2(F)$, the square-summable complex functions on the set $F$ of finitely supported configurations.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.configSpace\_def}}

\section*{The local embeddings}
For a finite set of sites $X$, the local embedding $\iota_X$ is a unital $*$-homomorphism from the matrices on the letters of $X$; this is part of the type of $\iota_X$ in the Lean statements. Statement 10 determines $\iota_X$ by its matrix elements.

\paragraph{10.} For a finite set of sites $X$, a matrix $m$ on the letters of $X$ and finitely supported configurations $s, t$, the matrix element $\langle e_s, \iota_X(m) e_t\rangle$ in the standard basis of $\ell^2(F)$ is $m(s|_X, t|_X)$ if $s$ and $t$ agree at every site outside $X$, and $0$ otherwise.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.inner\_localEmbed}}

\paragraph{11.} For a finite set of sites $X$, the local embedding $\iota_X$ is injective and $\|\iota_X(m)\| = \|m\|$ for every matrix $m$ on the letters of $X$, the norm of $m$ being its operator norm on $\ell^2$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.localEmbed\_injective\_and\_norm}}

\paragraph{12.} Compatibility: for finite sets of sites $X \subseteq Y$ and a matrix $m$ on the letters of $X$, $\iota_Y(m \otimes 1) = \iota_X(m)$, where \[(m \otimes 1)(\zeta, \zeta') = m(\zeta|_X, \zeta'|_X)\] if the letters $\zeta, \zeta'$ on $Y$ agree outside $X$, and $0$ otherwise.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.localEmbed\_extend}}

\paragraph{13.} Local commutativity: for disjoint finite sets of sites $X$ and $Y$, $\iota_X(a)$ and $\iota_Y(b)$ commute for all matrices $a$ on the letters of $X$ and $b$ on the letters of $Y$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.commute\_localEmbed\_of\_disjoint}}

\section*{The net of local algebras}
Every set of sites $O$ has a $*$-subalgebra $\mathcal A(O)$ of the bounded operators on $H$, closed in norm (statement 14), and the quasi-local algebra is $\mathcal A_{ql} = \mathcal A(\Lambda)$.

\paragraph{14.} For every set of sites $O$, the algebra $\mathcal A(O)$ is the norm closure, in the bounded operators on $H$, of the set of operators $\iota_X(m)$ with $X \subseteq O$ a finite set of sites and $m$ a matrix on the letters of $X$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.quasiLocalNet\_eq\_closure}}

\paragraph{15.} The quasi-local algebra $\mathcal A_{ql}$ is the algebra $\mathcal A(\Lambda)$ of the set of all sites.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.quasiLocalAlgebra\_def}}

\paragraph{16.} Local commutativity: for disjoint sets of sites $O_1$ and $O_2$, every element of $\mathcal A(O_1)$ commutes with every element of $\mathcal A(O_2)$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.commute\_of\_mem\_quasiLocalNet}}

\paragraph{17.} Isotony: for sets of sites $O_1 \subseteq O_2$, $\mathcal A(O_1) \subseteq \mathcal A(O_2)$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.quasiLocalNet\_mono}}

\section*{Symmetries and the reference configuration}
\paragraph{18.} Covariance: let a group $\Gamma$ act on the sites, with one finite alphabet $Q$ at every site and a reference configuration $s_0$ with $s_0(gu) = s_0(u)$. For each $g \in \Gamma$ there is a unitary $U$ on $H$ with $U \iota_X(m) U^* = \iota_{gX}(g_* m)$ for every finite set of sites $X$, where $gX = \{y : g^{-1} y \in X\}$ and \[(g_* m)(\zeta, \zeta') = m(\zeta \circ g, \zeta' \circ g),\] and with $U \mathcal A(O) U^* = \mathcal A(gO)$ for every set of sites $O$. The unitary is asserted for each $g$ separately; the statement does not say that $g \mapsto U$ is a representation.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.exists\_unitary\_covariance}}

\paragraph{19.} Independence of the reference configuration: for any two reference configurations $s_0$ and $s_0'$ (which may differ at infinitely many sites) there is a $*$-isomorphism $\varphi : \mathcal A_{ql} \to \mathcal A_{ql}'$ of the quasi-local algebras with $a \in \mathcal A(O) \iff \varphi(a) \in \mathcal A'(O)$ for every set of sites $O$ and every $a \in \mathcal A_{ql}$, and with $\varphi(\iota_X(m)) = \iota'_X(m)$ for every finite set of sites $X$ and matrix $m$ on its letters.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.exists\_starAlgEquiv\_of\_ref}}

\section*{The universal property}
\paragraph{20.} Universal property: let $D$ be a C$^*$-algebra and $(\beta_X)$ a family, indexed by the finite sets of sites $X$, of unital $*$-homomorphisms from the matrices on the letters of $X$ to $D$, compatible in the sense $\beta_Y(m \otimes 1) = \beta_X(m)$ for $X \subseteq Y$ (with $m \otimes 1$ as in the compatibility of the local embeddings). Then there is exactly one unital $*$-homomorphism $\Phi : \mathcal A_{ql} \to D$ with $\Phi(\iota_X(m)) = \beta_X(m)$ for all $X$ and $m$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.exists\_unique\_lift}}

\paragraph{21.} Let $D$ be a C$^*$-algebra other than the zero algebra and $(\beta_X)$ a compatible family as in the universal property. Then the $*$-homomorphism $\Phi : \mathcal A_{ql} \to D$ with $\Phi(\iota_X(m)) = \beta_X(m)$ for all $X$ and $m$ is an isometry.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.isometry\_lift}}

\section*{Finite volume}
\paragraph{22.} If the set of sites $\Lambda$ is finite, the quasi-local algebra $\mathcal A_{ql}$ is the algebra of all bounded operators on $H$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.quasiLocalAlgebra\_eq\_top\_of\_finite}}

\paragraph{23.} For a finite set of sites $O$, the algebra $\mathcal A(O)$ is the range of the local embedding $\iota_O$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.quasiLocalNet\_eq\_range\_of\_finite}}

\section*{Examples}
These examples show that the hypotheses of the main theorems can be met and that the objects are not degenerate.

\paragraph{24.} For the chain $\mathbb N$ with two letters per site and the reference configuration $0$, the quasi-local algebra is not the zero algebra.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.witness\_nontrivial\_quasiLocalAlgebra}}

\paragraph{25.} For the chain $\mathbb N$ with two letters per site and the reference configuration $0$, the set of finitely supported configurations, which indexes the basis of $H$, is infinite.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.witness\_infinite\_finConfig}}

\paragraph{26.} For the chain $\mathbb N$ with two letters per site and the reference configuration $0$, the algebra $\mathcal A(\{0\})$ of the site $0$ contains an element that is not a multiple of the identity.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.witness\_quasiLocalNet\_singleton\_not\_scalar}}

\paragraph{27.} For the chain $\mathbb Z$ with two letters per site and the reference configuration $0$, which is invariant under translations, there is a unitary $U$ on $H$ with $U \mathcal A(\{0\}) U^* = \mathcal A(\{1\})$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.witness\_translation\_covariant}}

\paragraph{28.} For the chain $\mathbb N$ with two letters per site, the reference configurations $0$ and $1$ differ at infinitely many sites, and there is a $*$-isomorphism $\varphi$ of their quasi-local algebras with $a \in \mathcal A(O) \iff \varphi(a) \in \mathcal A'(O)$ for every set of sites $O$.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.witness\_references\_differing\_everywhere}}

\paragraph{29.} For two sites $0, 1$ with two letters each, there is a monotone family $O \mapsto G(O)$, over the sets of sites, of subsets of the qudit algebra such that every $G(O)$ is closed under the adjoint, every element of $G(O_1)$ commutes with every element of $G(O_2)$ whenever $O_1$ and $O_2$ are orthogonal for the region index of the sets of sites, the norm closure of the star subalgebra generated by the union of all $G(O)$ is the whole qudit algebra, and $G(\{0\})$ contains two elements that do not commute.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.witness\_generated\_net\_hypotheses}}

\paragraph{30.} For two sites $0$ and $1$ carrying $2$ and $3$ letters, each of the algebras $\mathcal A(\{0\})$ and $\mathcal A(\{1\})$ of the qudit net contains two elements that do not commute.\par{\footnotesize\texttt{Sealed.witness\_qudit\_net\_noncommutative}}
