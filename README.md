# LocalAlgebrasLean

The kinematics of quantum systems with regions, formalized in Lean 4 over Mathlib: nets of local
algebras, the qudit net of a quantum spin system, and the quasi-local algebra of an infinite
lattice.

A quantum system with regions assigns to each region `O` an algebra `𝒜(O)` of the observables
measurable in `O`; larger regions carry larger algebras, and observables of separated regions
commute. For a lattice of `d`-level systems (qudits), `𝒜(X)` is the matrix algebra of the sites in
a finite region `X`, and the observables of the infinite lattice form the **quasi-local algebra**,
the norm closure of the union of the local algebras. The library constructs these objects once,
so that formalizations of quantum field theory, quantum cellular automata, propagation bounds and
tensor networks share one definition of each.

## What is proved

| | Statement | Lean files |
|---|---|---|
| Regions and nets | Regions with an orthogonality relation and their isomorphisms; nets of local algebras (isotonous assignments of closed star subalgebras of a C*-algebra); generation, local commutativity and covariance; nets generated by sets; commuting generators give a locally commutative net | `Region/Basic`, `Net/Basic`, `Net/Generated` |
| Sites and symmetries | The regions of any set of sites, orthogonal when disjoint; a group acting on the sites acts on regions, `O ↦ gO`, as automorphisms | `Lattice/Basic` |
| The qudit net | Configurations with site-dependent finite alphabets; the local algebra of a region as the matrices local to it; the net of local algebras, locally commutative; relabelling covariance | `Qudit/Config`, `Qudit/Relabel` |
| The quasi-local algebra | For a reference configuration `s₀`, the Hilbert space `ℓ²(F)` of the configurations differing from `s₀` at finitely many sites; the embedding of the matrices of a finite region, a unital, injective, isometric star homomorphism, compatible with `m ↦ m ⊗ 1`; the quasi-local net (closed, locally commutative) and the quasi-local algebra, in which the net is generating; agreement with the qudit net in finite volume; covariance under symmetries of the sites; independence of the reference configuration | `Qudit/QuasiLocal`, `QuasiLocalNet`, `QuasiLocalCov` |
| Universal property | Every compatible family of unital star homomorphisms out of the local matrix algebras into a C*-algebra extends uniquely through the quasi-local algebra, isometrically when the target is nonzero; hence any construction with compatible embeddings of dense union (for instance the abstract inductive limit) is isomorphic to this one by a unique isomorphism | `Qudit/QuasiLocalUniversal` |

No proof is left unfinished, no axiom is added, and no step is delegated to unchecked computation.
The statements can be checked without reading any proof (see Verification).

## Verification

The content of the library is stated in 21 theorems, listed in [`verify/CORE.md`](verify/CORE.md)
(also `verify/core.pdf`). Eleven of them are the main results above. Five describe, in terms of
Mathlib's definitions, the five definitions of this library on which the main results depend. The
other five are examples on the chains `ℕ` and `ℤ` with two letters per site, in which the
hypotheses of the main results hold and the algebras are not trivial. Since a statement depends
only on the definitions it involves, these 21 statements determine what the library asserts.

The file `verify/Challenge.lean` states the same 21 theorems in Lean without proofs, and
`verify/core.pdf` is generated from it; `verify/Solution.lean` proves them from the library. The
command

```
lake comparator --config verify/comparator.json --paranoid
```

checks that each theorem proved in `Solution.lean` has exactly the statement given in
`Challenge.lean`, that the proofs use no axioms other than `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`, and that Lean's kernel and four independently written proof checkers all accept
the proofs. To accept the results it is therefore enough to read the 21 statements. On Linux the
command compiles the code in a sandbox; on other systems the option `--inadvisably-no-sandbox` is
needed, and the compiled code then runs without that isolation. The scripts
`verify/check_same_statements.py` and `verify/make_core_page.py --check` confirm, in a few
seconds, that the two Lean files state the same theorems and that the page is generated from the
current `Challenge.lean`.

`lake comparator` is the proof judge maintained by the Lean developers
([leanprover/comparator](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator)), available as a command of
Lean's build tool `lake` from Lean 4.35. It is meant for proofs whose author is not trusted. The
Lean reference manual, in its chapter
[Validating a Lean Proof](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/ValidatingProofs/#validating-comparator),
presents it as the strongest of its levels of checking, intended among other cases for proofs
produced by AI. The judge takes the statements from a file fixed in advance (here
`Challenge.lean`), exports the declarations of both files to a plain text format, and compares
them there; it does not load the compiled library into its own process. With `--paranoid` it also
runs four further proof checkers, written independently of Lean's kernel, among them lean4lean
([digama0/lean4lean](https://github.com/digama0/lean4lean)), written in Lean, and nanoda
([ammkrn/nanoda_lib](https://github.com/ammkrn/nanoda_lib)), written in Rust; a proof is accepted
only if every checker accepts it. The judge takes as given what `Challenge.lean` imports: Lean's
own library, Mathlib, and the five definitions of this library on which the statements depend,
which is why the statements include descriptions of those five definitions in Mathlib's terms.

## The construction

The quasi-local algebra is realized concretely on `ℓ²` of the finitely supported configurations,
which is the representation of the product state of `s₀`. A matrix unit of a finite region acts
there as a partial permutation of the configurations, a bounded operator of norm at most one, and
the embedding of a local matrix is a finite sum of such operators. Mathlib provides Hilbert spaces
and bounded operators but no C*-inductive limits; the universal property shows that the concrete
algebra is, up to a unique isomorphism, the one any other construction yields.

## Build and verify

With the Lean installer `elan` (which reads the Lean version from `lean-toolchain`):

```
git clone https://github.com/etjoa002/LocalAlgebrasLean
cd LocalAlgebrasLean
lake exe cache get          # download the precompiled Mathlib
lake build LocalAlgebrasLean
lake comparator --config verify/comparator.json --paranoid   # see "Verification"
```

## Numerical checks

`checks/quasilocal_check.py` (Python with NumPy; `checks/requirements.txt`) tests the
finite-dimensional content of the construction on small windows of sites: the star-homomorphism
properties of the local embedding, compatibility with `m ⊗ 1`, commutation for disjoint regions,
covariance, and independence of the reference configuration, in exact integer arithmetic; operator
norms and ranks are compared with a tolerance of `10⁻⁹`. It also computes, on finite windows, the
distance `1/2` from the projection onto the reference state to the local algebra of every proper
region, which on the infinite lattice is a candidate for a later theorem (see Status).

## Conventions

C*-algebras are unital and star subalgebras contain the unit. A group acts on regions by
`gO = {x : g⁻¹x ∈ O}`. Operators of the quasi-local algebra act on `ℓ²` of the finitely supported
configurations. See `CONVENTIONS.md` for the full list.

## Scope

The library is kinematic: it constructs the algebras attached to regions, and dynamics belongs to
the libraries built on it. Any two reference configurations give isomorphic quasi-local nets,
through a relabelling of the letters at each site, and configurations that differ at finitely
many sites give the same representation on `ℓ²`.

## Status

The library is under development, and this repository is updated as the work progresses. Problem
reports and suggestions are welcome; see `CONTRIBUTING.md`. Planned next are nets of graded
algebras, in which odd observables of disjoint regions anticommute, as fermionic lattice systems
require, and lecture notes giving an account of the library in mathematical prose. Candidates for
later theorems: the representations on `ℓ²` for reference configurations
that differ at infinitely many sites are inequivalent (J. von Neumann, "On infinite direct
products", Compositio Math. 6 (1939) 1–77); and on the infinite lattice the projection onto the
reference state lies at distance `1/2` from the local algebra of every proper region.
