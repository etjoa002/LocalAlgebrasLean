# LocalAlgebrasLean

The kinematics of quantum systems with regions, formalized in Lean 4 over Mathlib: nets of local
algebras, the qudit net of a quantum spin system, and the quasi-local algebra of an infinite
lattice.

A quantum system with regions assigns to each region `O` an algebra `𝒜(O)` of the observables
measurable in `O`; larger regions carry larger algebras, and observables of separated regions
commute. For a lattice of `d`-level systems (qudits), `𝒜(X)` is the matrix algebra of the sites in
a finite region `X`, and the observables of the infinite lattice form the **quasi-local algebra**,
the norm closure of the union of the local algebras. This library builds these objects once, so
that work on quantum field theory, quantum cellular automata, propagation bounds and tensor
networks can rest on one definition of each. It knows nothing of dynamics.

## What is proved

| | Statement | Lean files |
|---|---|---|
| Regions and nets | Regions with an orthogonality relation and their isomorphisms; nets of local algebras (isotonous assignments of closed star subalgebras of a C*-algebra); generation, local commutativity and covariance; nets generated by sets; commuting generators give a locally commutative net | `Region/Basic`, `Net/Basic`, `Net/Generated` |
| Sites and symmetries | The regions of any set of sites, orthogonal when disjoint; a group acting on the sites acts on regions, `O ↦ gO`, as automorphisms | `Lattice/Basic` |
| The qudit net | Configurations with site-dependent finite alphabets; the local algebra of a region as the matrices local to it; the net of local algebras, locally commutative; relabelling covariance | `Qudit/Config`, `Qudit/Relabel` |
| The quasi-local algebra | For a reference configuration `s₀`, the Hilbert space `ℓ²(F)` of the configurations differing from `s₀` at finitely many sites; the embedding of the matrices of a finite region, a unital, injective, isometric star homomorphism, compatible with `m ↦ m ⊗ 1`; the quasi-local net (closed, locally commutative) and the quasi-local algebra, in which the net is generating; agreement with the qudit net in finite volume; covariance under symmetries of the sites; independence of the reference configuration | `Qudit/QuasiLocal`, `QuasiLocalNet`, `QuasiLocalCov` |
| Universal property | Every compatible family of unital star homomorphisms out of the local matrix algebras into a C*-algebra extends uniquely through the quasi-local algebra, isometrically when the target is nonzero; hence any construction with compatible embeddings of dense union (for instance the abstract inductive limit) is isomorphic to this one by a unique isomorphism | `Qudit/QuasiLocalUniversal` |

Every proof is complete: there are no unfinished proofs, no axioms added, and no steps delegated to
unchecked computation. The statements can be checked without reading any proof; see below.

## Check it yourself

The meaning of a theorem depends only on its statement and on the definitions the statement uses;
proofs and intermediate lemmas affect only whether it is true. The library is therefore summarized
by 21 statements, on one page: [`verify/CORE.md`](verify/CORE.md) (also `verify/core.pdf`). They are
the headline theorems, stated as far as possible in Mathlib's own vocabulary; three
characterizations that pin down, in Mathlib's terms, the five definitions of this library they use;
and five concrete instances showing that their hypotheses can be met and their objects are not
degenerate. The page is generated from `verify/Challenge.lean`, which states the same 21 theorems
in Lean without proofs (`python verify/make_core_page.py --check` confirms that the page matches the
file). `verify/Solution.lean` repeats each statement with the identical text and proves it from the
library. The judge `lake comparator`, part of Lean, settles the rest mechanically: each named
theorem has exactly the same statement in both files, as a fully elaborated term; the proofs use
only the three standard axioms of Lean's logic (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`); and
Lean's kernel, together with four independently written checkers, accepts every proof. What a
reader still has to judge is the one page; the proofs, the lemmas and the rest of the library
need not be read or trusted:

```
lake comparator --config verify/comparator.json --paranoid
```

On Linux the judge builds the code in a sandbox, which it needs to be fully trustworthy; on other
systems add `--inadvisably-no-sandbox`. `python verify/check_same_statements.py` checks the same
correspondence on the text of the two files, in a second.

## How it works

The quasi-local algebra is realized concretely on `ℓ²` of the finitely supported configurations,
which is the representation of the product state of `s₀`. A matrix unit of a finite region acts
there as a partial permutation of the configurations, a bounded operator of norm at most one, and
the embedding of a local matrix is a finite sum of such operators. Mathlib provides Hilbert spaces
and bounded operators but no C*-inductive limits; the universal property shows that the concrete
algebra is, up to a unique isomorphism, the one any other construction yields.

## Build and verify

With the Lean installer `elan` (which reads the Lean version from `lean-toolchain`):

```
git clone https://github.com/etjoa002/LocalAlgebrasLean
cd LocalAlgebrasLean
lake exe cache get          # download the precompiled Mathlib
lake build LocalAlgebrasLean
lake comparator --config verify/comparator.json --paranoid   # see "Check it yourself"
```

## Numerical checks

`checks/quasilocal_check.py` (Python with NumPy; `checks/requirements.txt`) tests the
finite-dimensional content of the construction on small windows of sites: the star-homomorphism
properties of the local embedding, compatibility with `m ⊗ 1`, commutation for disjoint regions,
covariance, and independence of the reference configuration, in exact integer arithmetic; operator
norms and ranks are compared with a tolerance of `10⁻⁹`. It also illustrates, on finite windows,
that the projection onto the reference state lies at distance `1/2` from every local algebra of a
proper region; the infinite-volume statement is not tested.

## Conventions

C*-algebras are unital and star subalgebras contain the unit. A group acts on regions by
`gO = {x : g⁻¹x ∈ O}`. Operators of the quasi-local algebra act on `ℓ²` of the finitely supported
configurations. See `CONVENTIONS.md` for the full list.

## Scope

The library is kinematic: it constructs the algebras attached to regions and leaves dynamics to
the libraries that use it. Any two reference configurations give isomorphic quasi-local nets,
through a relabelling of the letters at each site, and configurations that differ at finitely
many sites give the same representation on `ℓ²`. Configurations that differ at infinitely many
sites give inequivalent representations (J. von Neumann, "On infinite direct products",
Compositio Math. 6 (1939) 1–77); that comparison is not part of the library.
